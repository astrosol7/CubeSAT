1. PROJECT CORE IDEA
The project is a proposed:
Rapid Disaster Response and Situational Awareness System
The system is inspired by CubeSat and aerospace engineering principles but should not automatically be treated as a real orbital satellite.
The central idea is to create a rapidly deployable autonomous sensing platform that can be activated after a disaster occurs and collect localized, time-sensitive information to support emergency responders.
The project focuses specifically on the Response stage of disaster management.
It is NOT primarily a disaster prediction system.
It is NOT simply another environmental monitoring system.
Its purpose is to answer a critical operational question after a disaster occurs:
What happened, where is it happening, how severe is it, what areas are affected, and what information do emergency responders need immediately?
The system should provide rapid situational awareness.

3. IMPORTANT PROJECT DECISION
The team initially considered building a disaster detection or prediction system.
This direction was challenged.
The reason is that significant remote-sensing and environmental data may already be available from existing satellite systems and organizations, including systems that provide Earth observation data.
Therefore, simply building another system that measures:
Temperature
Images
Environmental conditions
and calling it "disaster detection" would not create a strong engineering contribution.
The project should instead identify an operational gap.
The proposed gap is:
Existing large-scale monitoring and remote-sensing systems may identify or report a disaster, but emergency responders may still require rapid, localized, on-demand situational information immediately after the event.
Therefore:
Existing data identifies or provides awareness of the broad event.
The proposed system provides:
Rapid local situational awareness to support emergency response decisions.
This distinction is critical.
Do not lose it.
